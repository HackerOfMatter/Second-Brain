## 🧭 Executive Summary

- **Core concept:** This chapter explains how to identify, forecast, and evaluate _project cash flows_ to make long-term investment decisions through _capital budgeting_.
    
- **Why it matters:** The success of every investment decision depends on accurate estimation of _incremental cash flows_ and proper evaluation using tools like NPV, IRR, and PI.
    
- **Real-world relevance:** Corporations like Apple and Boeing rely on these techniques to decide whether to expand, acquire, or launch new products.
    
- **Likely use:** Tests, applied projects, case analyses, and scenario modeling exercises.

## 🧩 Key Concepts & Terms

| Term / Concept                     | Definition / Meaning                                                 | Example or Context                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Incremental Cash Flows**         | Only the cash flows that change _because_ of a project.              | Launching a new product reduces old sales → loss is a relevant cost. |
| **Pro Forma Financial Statements** | Forecasted income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. | Used to project OCF and FCF for a project.                           |
| **Operating Cash Flow (OCF)**      | EBIT + Depreciation – Taxes                                          | Measures true cash from operations.                                  |
| **Net Capital Spending (NCS)**     | ΔNet Fixed Assets + Depreciation                                     | Captures spending on long-term assets.                               |
| **Changes in NWC**                 | ΔCurrent Assets – ΔCurrent Liabilities                               | Additional short-term investment needed for a project.               |
| **Free Cash Flow (FCF)**           | OCF – NCS – ΔNWC                                                     | Total cash available to investors after operations and reinvestment. |
| **NPV (Net Present Value)**        | Present value of inflows – cost                                      | Determines whether a project creates value.                          |
| **Scenario Analysis**              | Evaluation of best, base, and worst-case outcomes                    | Measures risk of forecast assumptions.                               |
| **Capital Rationing**              | When limited funds force prioritization among positive-NPV projects. | Used when company can’t fund all profitable opportunities.           |
|                                    |                                                                      |                                                                      |

## 📊 Frameworks, Models, or Equations

> Use this for ratios, decision models, or processes.

|Model / Formula|Components|How to Interpret|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|**OCF = EBIT + Depreciation – Taxes**|Operating income and noncash expense|Core driver of project inflows|OCF = 120,000 + 30,000 – 24,000 = 126,000|
|**NCS = ΔNet Fixed Assets + Depreciation**|Tracks spending on long-term assets|Indicates investment size|NCS = 250,000 + 30,000 = 280,000|
|**ΔNWC = ΔCA – ΔCL**|Current Assets – Current Liabilities|Reflects tied-up working capital|20,000 – 10,000 = 10,000|
|**FCF = OCF – NCS – ΔNWC**|Cash after reinvestment|Measures value creation potential|FCF = 126,000 – 280,000 – 10,000|
|**NPV = Σ(CFt / (1 + r)^t) – Initial Cost**|Time value of money|If NPV > 0 → accept|NPV = +25,000 ✅|
|**PI = PV of Inflows / Initial Investment**|Value per dollar invested|PI > 1 = Accept|PI = 1.25|
|**Break-Even Analysis**|Sales or cost point where NPV = 0|Sensitivity test|BE Sales = 2,000 units|

**Common formulas to record clearly:**

- ROI = (Gain – Cost) / Cost
    
- Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
    
- Break-even Point = Fixed Costs / (Price – Variable Cost)

## 🪜 Step-by-Step Application (Class Walkthrough)

> Capture the professor’s worked example or logic.

1. **Given / Known:** Forecasted project revenues, costs, depreciation, and investment in equipment and working capital.
    
2. **Formula or Framework:** Use OCF → NCS → ΔNWC → FCF → NPV.
    
3. **Substitution:** Insert forecasted values into cash flow formulas.
    
4. **Solution:** Calculate annual FCFs, then discount them to present using the required return (WACC).
    
5. **Interpretation (what it means):**
    
    - NPV > 0 → Accept project (creates shareholder value).
        
    - Compare alternative projects by NPV, PI, and IRR.
        

> 💡 _Takeaway:_  
> “Cash flow, not accounting profit, determines value creation. Every project’s worth is found in its incremental, after-tax cash flows.”

## 🔍 Conceptual Deep Dive

- **Assumptions behind the model:**
    
    - Forecasted sales, cost growth rates, and discount rate are realistic.
        
    - Inflation is consistently applied to both inflows and discount rate.
        
    - Taxes and depreciation follow accurate accounting rules.
        
- **What changes if X variable shifts:**
    
    - Increase in costs → Lower OCF → Lower NPV.
        
    - Higher salvage value → Higher terminal cash flow.
        
    - Delay in cash inflows → Reduced NPV due to time value of money.
        
- **Common pitfalls / student errors:**
    
    - Including sunk costs.
        
    - Ignoring opportunity costs or side effects.
        
    - Forgetting to add back depreciation.
        
    - Using inconsistent inflation assumptions.

## 🔄 Quick Review (Retrieval Section)

- **1 key question:** What is the relationship between FCF and NPV in capital budgeting?
    
- **1 sample calculation:**
    
    > FCF = 150,000 – 80,000 – 10,000 = 60,000; NPV = 60,000 / 1.1³ – 120,000 = +5,000 ✅
    
- **1 misconception to avoid:** Thinking profit = cash flow. Depreciation and working capital changes matter.
    
- **1 mini-summary:** Project evaluation = _Estimate incremental cash flows → Discount to PV → Make NPV-based decision_.
