### **1. Core Objective of Financial Management**

- The main goal is **to maximize the current value per share** of the firm’s existing stock.
    
- For privately held firms, the goal is to maximize the **market value of the owners’ equity**.
    
- Focus on **long-term value creation**, not short-term profit spikes.
    

---

### **2. Forms of Business Organization**

| Type                                | Key Features                  | Pros                                     | Cons                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Sole Proprietorship**             | Owned by one person           | Simple, owner keeps profits              | Unlimited liability, limited capital       |
| **Partnership**                     | Two or more owners            | Shared skills/resources                  | Joint liability, limited life              |
| **Corporation**                     | Separate legal entity         | Limited liability, easy to raise capital | Double taxation, regulatory burden         |
| **LLC (Limited Liability Company)** | Hybrid of corp. + partnership | Limited liability, single taxation       | More complex to form than sole/partnership |
    

---

### **3. Financial Statements Overview**

| Statement               | Purpose                     | Key Focus                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Balance Sheet**       | Snapshot at a point in time | _Assets = Liabilities + Equity_                                                                                        |
| **Income Statement**    | Performance over a period   | _Revenues − Expenses = Net Income_                                                                                     |
| **Cash Flow Statement** | Sources/uses of cash        | Split into: • **Operating** – day-to-day • **Investing** – long-term assets • **Financing** – debt/equity transactions |

### **4. Financial Ratios**

Understand what each ratio reveals about a company:

**Liquidity Ratios**

| Ratio             | Formula                                            | Meaning                                |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Current Ratio** | Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities               | Can the firm cover short-term debts?   |
| **Quick Ratio**   | (Current Assets − Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities | Liquidity without relying on inventory |
    

**Profitability Ratios**

| Ratio             | Formula                   | Insight                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Profit Margin** | Net Income ÷ Sales        | Profit from each $1 of sales |
| **ROA**           | Net Income ÷ Total Assets | Efficiency in using assets   |
| **ROE**           | Net Income ÷ Equity       | Return to shareholders       |
    

**Leverage Ratios**

| Ratio                     | Formula                   | Insight                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Debt Ratio**            | Total Debt ÷ Total Assets | % of assets financed by debt         |
| **Times Interest Earned** | EBIT ÷ Interest Expense   | Ability to meet interest obligations |
    

**Efficiency Ratios**

| Ratio                  | Formula              | Insight                               |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Inventory Turnover** | COGS ÷ Inventory     | How fast inventory sells              |
| **Asset Turnover**     | Sales ÷ Total Assets | How efficiently assets generate sales |

### **6. Interest Rates and Compounding (Investment Descisions)**
	Always compare benefits ($ inflow) vs cost ($ outflows) over time

| Method                            | Formula                                         | Decision Rule                       | Insight                            |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Net Present Value (NPV)**       | `NPV = Σ [CFₜ / (1 + r)ᵗ] – Initial Investment` | Accept if **NPV > 0**               | Measures direct dollar value added |
| **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** | Solve for `r` when **NPV = 0**                  | Accept if **IRR > required return** | % return on project                |
| **Payback Period**                | Years until cash inflows = investment           | Shorter = better                    | Ignores time value of money        |
| **Profitability Index (PI)**      | `PI = PV of future CF / Initial investment`     | Accept if **PI > 1**                | Efficiency of investment           |

### **7. Bond Basics**

> 💭 _Core idea:_ Investors require **higher returns** for **higher risk**.

#### ⚙️ **Key Measures**

- **Expected Return:**  
    `E(R) = Σ [pᵢ × Rᵢ]`  
    (_weighted average of possible outcomes_)
    
- **Variance:**  
    `σ² = Σ [pᵢ × (Rᵢ – E(R))²]`
    
- **Standard Deviation:**  
    `σ = √σ²`  
    (_measures total risk/volatility_)

### **9. Capital Budgeting**

**Key Evaluation Methods:**

- **Net Present Value (NPV):** Preferred method; measures added value.
    
    - Accept project if NPV > 0.
        
- **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** Discount rate where NPV = 0.
    
- **Payback Period:** Time to recover the initial investment (simple but limited).

### Cost of Capital (financing descisions)
	Finding the minimum acceptable return a firm must earn to satisfy investors

|Type|Formula|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|**Cost of Debt (after-tax)**|`Rd × (1 – Tax Rate)`|Use **YTM** if bonds trade at a discount/premium|
|**Cost of Equity**|via CAPM → `Re = Rf + β (Rm – Rf)`|Required return by shareholders|
|**Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)**|`WACC = (E/V)Re + (D/V)Rd(1 – Tc)`|Weighted blend of debt & equity|

### **11. Leverage**

|Type|Definition|Effect|
|---|---|---|
|**Operating Leverage**|Fixed costs amplify changes in sales|Higher leverage → higher risk|
|**Financial Leverage**|Use of debt to finance assets|Increases ROE, but also risk of default|

> ⚖️ _Balance:_ Leverage magnifies both gains **and** losses — firms must manage it carefully.

---

### **12. Dividend Policy**

|Policy|Description|Effect|
|---|---|---|
|**Constant Payout Ratio**|Fixed % of earnings|Dividends rise/fall with profits|
|**Stable Dollar Dividend**|Fixed $ per share|Investors value predictability|
|**Residual Policy**|Pay dividends only if earnings > project needs|Prioritizes reinvestment|

> 💭 _Think:_ Dividends vs Retention trade-off — reinvested earnings drive **sustainable growth**.
