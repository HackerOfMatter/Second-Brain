# 🧠 Core Idea

> ⚡ **In essence:** A breach occurs when a party fails to perform contractual duties, and remedies seek to restore the injured party to the position they would have been in had the contract been performed.

# 🔍 Key Concepts

- **Definition(s):**
    
    - **Breach of Contract** — violation of any term or duty in a legally binding contract.
        
    - **Remedy** — legal means to compensate or enforce rights after breach.
        
- **Principles:**
    
    - Remedies aim for **expectation interest** — giving the nonbreaching party the “benefit of the bargain.”
        
    - The type of breach (material vs. minor) determines available remedies.
        
- **Types of Damages:**
    
    - **Compensatory** — direct damages covering actual losses.
        
    - **Consequential** — indirect damages resulting from special circumstances (e.g., lost profits).
        
    - **Punitive** — rare in contract law; meant to punish willful misconduct.
        
    - **Nominal** — symbolic damages awarded when no measurable loss occurred.
        
    - **Equitable Remedies:** [[Quantum Meruit (Equitable Damages)]], rescission, or specific performance.
        
- **Example:**
    
    - Seller fails to deliver machinery; buyer sues for cost difference between contract price and replacement purchase.
