# 🧠 Core Idea

> ⚡ **In essence:** Certain contracts must be written under the Statute of Frauds to prevent fraud and ensure evidence of agreement.

# 🔍 Key Concepts

- **Definition(s):**
    
    - **Suretyship** — a promise to answer for another person’s debt must be written.
        
    - **Real Estate** — sales, transfers, or long leases (usually >1 year) must be written.
        
    - **One-Year Rule** — if a contract _cannot possibly_ be performed within one year, it must be in writing.
        
    - **UCC Sales of Goods** — contracts for sale of goods over **$500** must be in writing under the Uniform Commercial Code.
        
- **Exceptions:**
    
    - **Partial performance** (esp. real estate — when possession or payment occurs).
        
    - **Admission in court** that a contract exists.
        
    - **Merchant confirmation rule** (UCC 2-201(2)): written confirmation between merchants satisfies the SOF.

# 🪞 Personal Note

If it’s big money, long term, or real estate — **write it down**. Courts won’t rely on “he said, she said.”
