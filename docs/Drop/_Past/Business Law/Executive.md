## 🔑 Article II, Section 1: Presidential Power

- **Power**: The President serves as the chief executive of the United States, responsible for ==enforcing federal laws and executing federal policies==
- **Key Terms**:
    - Chief Executive: The head of the executive branch.
    - Cabinet: A group of high-ranking officials who advise the President.

---

## 🔒 Article II, Section 2: Presidential Powers

- **Power**: The President has the ==power to appoint== ambassadors, other officers, and federal judges (subject to Senate confirmation)
- **Key Terms**:
    - Ambassadors: Representatives of the United States abroad.
    - Federal Judges: Judges who serve on the federal courts.

---

## 🔓 Article II, Section 3: Presidential Powers

- **Power**: The President has the power to ==grant reprieves and pardons== for federal offenses (except in cases of impeachment)
- **Key Terms**:
    - Reprieve: A temporary stay of execution.
    - Pardon: A formal forgiveness of a crime.

---

## 🔒 Article II, Section 4: Presidential Powers

- **Power**: The President has the power to ==make treaties== with foreign governments (subject to Senate ratification) and negotiate executive agreements
- **Key Terms**:
    - Treaties: Formal agreements between nations.
    - Executive Agreements: Informal agreements between the United States and another country.

---

## 🔓 Article II, Section 5: Presidential Powers

- **Power**: The President has the power to ==command the military== (subject to congressional approval of defense spending)
- **Key Terms**:
    - Commander-in-Chief: The President's role as the highest-ranking officer in the armed forces.
    - Defense Spending: Budget for the military.

---

## 🔒 Article II, Section 6: Presidential Powers

- **Power**: The President has the power to take care that the ==laws be faithfully executed== (i.e., enforcing federal laws)
- **Key Terms**:
    - Faithfully Executed: Carrying out the duties of the office with integrity and honesty.
    - Executive Orders: Written orders from the President directing executive branch agencies.
