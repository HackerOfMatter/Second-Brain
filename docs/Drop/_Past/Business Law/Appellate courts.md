# ⚖️ **Appellate Courts — Summary**

## 1. **Definition**

- An appellate court is a court that **reviews decisions made by lower (trial) courts**.
    
- They handle **appeals**, not new trials.
    

---

## 2. **What They Do**

- Review for **errors of law** (not fact).
    
- Check if the trial court:
    
    - Applied the law correctly.
        
    - Protected the parties’ constitutional rights.
        
    - Followed fair procedures.
        
- Can **affirm, reverse, modify, or remand** (send back) the lower court’s decision.
    

---

## 3. **What They Don’t Do**

- Do **not hear new evidence**.
    
- Do **not retry the case** or re-question witnesses.
    
- Only look at the **trial record** (transcripts, evidence admitted, motions).
    

---

## 4. **Structure in the U.S.**

- **State systems:** Trial courts → Intermediate appellate courts → State supreme court.
    
- **Federal system:** District courts → U.S. Courts of Appeals (Circuit Courts) → U.S. Supreme Court.
    

---

## 5. **Example**

- Trial court convicts someone.
    
- Defendant appeals, arguing the judge admitted illegal evidence.
    
- Appellate court reviews the record:
    
    - If correct → conviction affirmed.
        
    - If error → conviction reversed or case sent back for new trial.
        

---

✅ **Key Takeaway**  
Appellate courts = **“error-checkers”**.  
They don’t retry cases but ensure the law was applied fairly and correctly.
