**Summary:**  
Common law is **judge-made law** derived from **court decisions** rather than statutes. It relies on the principle of **stare decisis**, meaning courts follow precedent to ensure consistency and fairness.

**Key Points:**

- Developed through judicial opinions that interpret statutes, constitutions, or existing precedent.
    
- Provides flexibility by adapting to new issues not addressed by written law.
    
- Ensures predictability and stability in legal outcomes.
    

**Example:**

- _Donoghue v. Stevenson_ (1932) established modern negligence under common law principles.
    

**Compare:**

- [[Statutory Law]] → legislative.
    
- [[Administrative Law]] → regulatory.
    
- Common law often fills in gaps between the two.
