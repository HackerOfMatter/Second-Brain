# 📦 Perpetual Inventory System

## 🧠 Core Concept

The perpetual inventory system updates inventory records in real time — after every sale, purchase, or return.

Unlike the periodic system (which updates occasionally), perpetual systems always reflect the current quantity and cost of inventory on hand.

---

## 🔄 How It Works

- 📥 Purchases → Immediately added to inventory balance  
- 📤 Sales → Instantly reduce inventory and record cost of goods sold (COGS)  
- 📦 Inventory data stays current and accurate

🖥️ Typically used with POS (point-of-sale) software, barcodes, or RFID scanning.

---

## ✅ Example

Imagine a store sells 1 unit of an item for $30 that originally cost $10:

- Sale triggers 2 entries:
  - Revenue:  
    - Debit Cash $30  
    - Credit Sales $30  
  - Inventory:  
    - Debit COGS $10  
    - Credit Inventory $10

→ Inventory and cost are updated in real time.

---

## 📊 Advantages

+ Up-to-date inventory levels  
+ Better control over stockouts and shrinkage  
+ Instant COGS tracking  
+ Helps enable just-in-time (JIT) inventory systems

---

## ⚠️ Disadvantages

− More expensive systems (hardware/software)  
− Not practical for small businesses without tech  
− Requires constant accuracy (susceptible to data errors)

---

## 📌 Spaced Repetition Flashcards

Q: What is the main feature of the perpetual inventory system?  
A: It updates inventory records continuously after each transaction.

Q: What are two key benefits of using a perpetual inventory system?  
A: Real-time stock tracking and immediate COGS updates.

Q: What happens to inventory and COGS when a sale is made under the perpetual system?  
A: Inventory decreases, and COGS is immediately recorded.
