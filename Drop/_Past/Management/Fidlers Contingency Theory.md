**Leader-Member Relations:**

- How well do leaders understand and relate to their team members?
- Do they have a good rapport with them?

**Task Structure:**

- How structured is the task or problem at hand?
- Is it clear, predictable, and routine or complex, unpredictable, and novel?

The theory proposes that leadership effectiveness is determined by the combination of leader-member relations and task structure. There are four possible combinations:

1. **Leader-Centric**: Leader has high power, member relations are poor, and task is unstructured (novel). Effective leaders in this situation have a strong sense of self-efficacy, are charismatic, and can inspire their team to take on the challenge.
2. **Task-Centric**: Task is highly structured, member relations are good, but leader has low power. In this case, effective leaders are task-oriented, analytical, and can rely on their team's expertise to get the job done.
3. **Member-Centric**: Leader-member relations are strong, task is unstructured (novel), and leader has high power. Effective leaders in this situation prioritize building strong relationships with their team members, fostering trust, and empowering them to take ownership of the task.
4. **Hybrid**: Both leader-member relations and task structure are average. In this case, effective leaders are flexible, adaptable, and able to adjust their leadership style to suit the situation.

The theory suggests that a leader's effectiveness depends on how well they match their leadership style to the situation. Leaders who can adapt to different situations and use their strengths effectively are more likely to be successful.
