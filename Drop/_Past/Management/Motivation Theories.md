# Behavior

**Process theories** explain how workers select behavioral actions to meet their needs and determine their choice

## Equity theory
	J. Stacy Adams, workers compare the reward potential to the effort they must expend. Equity exists when workers perceive that rewards equal effort

## Expectancy theory
	Victor Vroom says employee will be motivated to exert a high level of effort when he or she believes that:

1. Effort will lead to a good performance appraisal.
2. A good appraisal will lead to organizational rewards.
3. The organizational rewards will satisfy his or her personal goals.

**Key:** to motivate workers, managers must strengthen workers' perceptions of their efforts as both possible and worthwhile, clarify expectations of performances, tie rewards to performances, and make sure that rewards are desirable.

## Reinforcement theory
	E. L. Thorndike's law of effect, simply looks at the relationship between behavior and its consequences. This theory focuses on modifying an employee's on‐the‐job behavior through the appropriate use of one of the following four techniques:

- **Positive reinforcement** rewards desirable behavior. Positive reinforcement  such as a pay raise or promotion, is provided as a reward for positive behavior with the intention of increasing the probability that the desired behavior will be repeated.
- **Avoidance** is an attempt to show an employee what the consequences of improper behavior will be. consequence
  -  **Punishment** (threats, docking pay, suspension) is an attempt to decrease the likelihood of a behavior recurring by applying negative consequences.

## Goal-setting theory
	Edwin Locke, proposed that intentions to work toward a goal are a major source of work motivation
	
- The employee must be committed to the goal.

- The employee must believe that he is capable of performing the task.

- Tasks involved in achieving the goal should be simple, familiar, and independent.

- The goal‐setting theory is culture bound and is popular in North American cultures.

managers need to work with their employees in determining goal objectives in order to provide targets for motivation and give feedback on performance
