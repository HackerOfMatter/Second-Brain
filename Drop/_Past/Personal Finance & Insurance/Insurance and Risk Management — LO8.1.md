**Source Type:** Textbook section / Lecture summary  
**Topic:** Identifying types of risks, methods of risk management, and developing a personal risk management plan

---

### **Key Takeaways**

- **Insurance** provides **financial protection** against possible losses due to events like illness, injury, or property damage.
    
- **Risk management** is a **systematic approach** to identifying and handling potential risks to reduce financial losses.
    
- There are **three main types of risks**: **personal**, **property**, and **liability**.
    
- **Four main methods** of managing risk: **avoidance, reduction, assumption, and shifting.**
    
- A **personal insurance plan** evolves with life stages, changing assets, and responsibilities.
    

---

### **Main Concepts & Theories**

#### **1. What Is Insurance?**

- **Definition:** Insurance is **protection against possible financial loss** through risk-sharing.
    
- **Parties Involved:**
    
    - **Insurer:** Company providing the protection.
        
    - **Policyholder:** Purchaser of the insurance policy.
        
    - **Insured:** Individuals or property covered under the policy.
        
- **Key Terms:**
    
    - **Premium:** Fee paid for insurance coverage.
        
    - **Policy:** Written contract detailing coverage terms.
        
    - **Coverage:** Protection provided by the policy.
        

---

#### **2. Types of Risk**

|**Type of Risk**|**Description**|**Example**|
|---|---|---|
|**Personal Risk**|Loss of income or life due to illness, disability, unemployment, or death.|Job loss due to injury.|
|**Property Risk**|Loss or damage to property due to perils (fire, theft, storms).|Home destroyed by fire.|
|**Liability Risk**|Losses caused by negligence leading to injury or property damage.|Visitor slips on icy steps.|

**Key Distinctions:**

- **Risk:** Chance of loss or injury.
    
- **Peril:** The cause of potential loss (e.g., fire, accident).
    
- **Hazard:** Anything that increases likelihood of a peril (e.g., faulty wiring).
    
- **Pure Risk:** Only chance of loss (insurable).
    
- **Speculative Risk:** Chance of loss _or_ gain (not insurable, e.g., investing).
    

---

#### **3. Risk Management Methods**

|**Method**|**Definition**|**Example**|**Notes**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Avoidance**|Eliminating exposure to a risk.|Not driving to avoid car accidents.|May involve opportunity loss.|
|**Reduction**|Taking precautions to reduce likelihood or impact.|Wearing seatbelts, installing smoke alarms.|Reduces but doesn’t eliminate risk.|
|**Assumption (Retention)**|Accepting responsibility for small risks.|Not insuring an old car.|Often combined with self-insurance (savings fund).|
|**Shifting (Transfer)**|Transferring risk to an insurer via policy.|Buying health, auto, or home insurance.|Deductibles combine assumption & shifting.|

### **Critical Analysis & Insights**

**Strengths:**

- Promotes proactive financial planning and protection.
    
- Encourages diversification of risk management strategies beyond insurance.
    
- Emphasizes adaptability — plans evolve with life stages.
    

**Weaknesses / Limitations:**

- Heavy reliance on insurance may discourage preventive behaviors.
    
- Premium costs can be prohibitive for low-income households.
    
- Self-insurance assumes availability of savings, not feasible for everyone.
    

**Implications:**

- Balanced risk management requires both **behavioral adjustments (prevention)** and **financial instruments (insurance).**
    
- Regular plan reviews ensure coverage remains relevant as assets, family size, and income change.
