## 🏛️ **Statutory Law**

**Summary:**  
Statutory law consists of **formal, written laws enacted by legislative bodies** such as Congress or state legislatures. These laws provide the **primary source of legal authority** in most modern legal systems.

**Key Points:**

- Found in **codes** (e.g., U.S. Code, California Penal Code).
    
- Originates through the **legislative process** — proposed as bills, debated, amended, and passed.
    
- Enforced by the **executive branch** and interpreted by the **judicial branch**.
    
- Often broad and general, leaving room for agencies to clarify details through [[Administrative Law]].
    

**Example:**

- The _Civil Rights Act of 1964_ (Title VII) prohibits employment discrimination — a classic statutory law.
    

**Compare:**

- [[Common Law / Case Law]] → developed by judges through precedent.
    
- [[Administrative Law]] → developed by agencies under delegated authority.
