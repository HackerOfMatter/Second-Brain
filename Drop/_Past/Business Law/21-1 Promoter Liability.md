# 21-1 Promoter Liability ⚖️

**At a Glance:**  
A *promoter* is someone who organizes a corporation before it legally exists. Promoters can be personally liable for pre-incorporation contracts unless specific steps are taken.

---

## Legal Foundation
- **Promoter Definition:** A person who takes the initiative to form a corporation—arranging capital, property, or contracts before incorporation.
- **Key Rule:** The corporation **does not yet exist** → so it **cannot be bound** by contracts.
- **Result:** The **promoter** is personally liable for any contracts made *before incorporation*, unless the contract says otherwise.

### 🧾 Liability Rules
1. **Before Incorporation:**  
   - Promoter **is personally liable** on all contracts made in the name of the future corporation.  
   - Corporation cannot ratify the contract because it didn’t exist yet.  

2. **After Incorporation:**  
   - Corporation can **adopt** the contract (by board approval or conduct).  
   - If adopted, the corporation becomes liable **along with** the promoter — *unless* the contract included a **novation** (a 3-party agreement releasing the promoter).  

3. **Novation:**  
   - Substitutes the corporation for the promoter, releasing the promoter from liability.  

---

## Common Exam Traps
- “Ratification” ≠ “Adoption.”  
  → Only *adoption* applies because the corp didn’t exist at the time.  
- Promoter still liable *unless* a novation occurs.  

---

## Example
Bianka signs a lease on behalf of “Bianka, Inc.” before incorporation.  
If the corporation later adopts the lease, **both** Bianka and Bianka, Inc. are liable unless the landlord agrees to release Bianka (novation).
