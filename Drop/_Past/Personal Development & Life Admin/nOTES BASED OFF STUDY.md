# **Comprehensive Law Study Guide**

---

## **1. Criminal Law**

- **Definition:** Body of law prohibiting conduct harmful to society; prosecuted by the state.
    
- **Key Principles:**
    
    - Burden of proof: **beyond a reasonable doubt** in criminal cases
        
    - Assault, theft, embezzlement, arson, hacking (e.g., shutting down networks) are crimes.
        
    - **Example:** Aaron burning his own warehouse to collect insurance = criminal fraud.
        
- **Defenses:**
    
    - Mistake of fact (sometimes applicable)
        
    - Lack of intent (mens rea)
        

---

## **2. Civil Law & Torts**

### **Negligence**

- Elements:
    
    1. **Duty of care** owed by defendant
        
    2. **Breach** of that duty
        
    3. **Causation** (actual + proximate)
        
    4. **Damages** suffered by plaintiff
        
- **Res Ipsa Loquitur:**
    
    - “The thing speaks for itself”
        
    - Plaintiff must prove:
        
        1. Event normally does not happen without negligence
            
        2. Defendant had control
            
        3. Plaintiff not at fault
            
- **Strict Liability / Ultrahazardous Activities:**
    
    - Liability without fault for inherently dangerous activities (e.g., explosives, wild animals, toxic chemicals)
        
- **Product Liability:**
    
    - Manufacturers strictly liable for defective or dangerous products
        
    - Breach of **implied warranty of merchantability** if goods unfit for ordinary use
        
    - Disclaimer must be clear to negate implied warranties
        
- **Defamation:**
    
    - False statement presented as fact
        
    - Communicated to a third party
        
    - Causes harm to reputation
        
    - Private statement may not qualify if not published
        
- **Privacy:**
    
    - Justice Brandeis emphasized privacy protects personal dignity and freedom

## **3. Contracts**

- **Formation:**
    
    - Offer + Acceptance + Consideration = Valid Contract
        
    - **Bilateral vs. Unilateral Contracts:**
        
        - Bilateral: exchange of promises
            
        - Unilateral: promise in exchange for performance
            
- **UCC Article 2:**
    
    - Governs **sale of goods**, not real estate or services
        
    - **Predominant purpose test:** Determine if contract primarily involves goods
        
- **Warranties & Disclaimers:**
    
    - **Express warranties:** Statements of fact about goods
        
    - **Implied warranty of merchantability:** Goods fit for ordinary purpose
        
    - Disclaimers must be conspicuous to be enforceable
        
- **Integration Clause / Merger Clause:**
    
    - Prevents parties from enforcing oral promises not included in written contract
        
- **Choice-of-Law Clause:**
    
    - Parties can choose governing law for disputes
        
- **Quantum Meruit:**
    
    - “As much as he/she deserves”
        
    - Recovery for services rendered when no contract exists
        
- **Common Contract Terms:**
    
    - Conditional covenant: obligation dependent on an event
        
    - Reciprocal promise: mutual promises exchanged
        

---

## **4. Secured Transactions**

- **Security Interest:** Legal right in collateral to secure debt repayment
    
- **Perfection:** Step to protect security interest against third parties
    
- **Collateral:** Property subject to a security interest
    
- **Example:** Inventory, equipment, accounts receivable
    
- **Effect of Bankruptcy / Sale:**
    
    - Perfected security interests generally maintain priority
        
    - Sale of collateral can complicate claims
        

---

## **5. Agency & Employment Law**

- **Duties of Agents:**
    
    - **Loyalty:** No self-dealing or conflict of interest
        
    - **Obedience:** Follow principal’s instructions
        
    - **Care / Diligence:** Reasonable skill in performing duties
        
    - **Accounting:** Keep accurate records of transactions
        
- **Scope of Employment / Liability:**
    
    - Employers liable for:
        
        - Physical torts committed within scope of employment
            
        - Nonphysical torts only if employee has authority
            
- **Termination of Agency / Employment:**
    
    - At-will relationships can be ended by either party
        
- **Unfair Labor Practices:**
    
    - Interfering with union formation
        
    - Coercion or promises to influence employee decisions
        
- **Employment Law Highlights:**
    
    - Fair Labor Standards Act: Minimum wage and overtime standards
        
    - ADA: Reasonable accommodation for disabilities
        
    - Title VII: Prohibits discrimination (including immigration status)
        
- **Examples:**
    
    - Lyle buying company property for personal gain = breach of loyalty
        
    - Karen taking clients = potential liability to former employer

## **6. Ethics**

- **Deontological Ethics:** Act according to duty/rules regardless of consequences
    
- **Categorical Imperative:** Decide based on universalizable principles
    
- **Utilitarian Ethics:** Act to maximize overall benefit; potential problem: individual rights may be sacrificed
    
- **Moral Universalism:** Apply ethical principles consistently to everyone
    
- **Moral Relativism:** Ethics depend on cultural or local norms
    
- **Corporate Ethics Examples:**
    
    - Decisions that benefit the company, employees, shareholders, and society
        

---

## **7. Constitutional & Government Law**

- **Three Branches of U.S. Government:** Legislative, Executive, Judicial
    
- **Separation of Powers:** Each branch has independent powers; line-item veto violates this principle
    
- **Statutes:** Must not conflict with Constitution; federal law supersedes state law
    
- **Executive Authority:** Cannot unilaterally create law (e.g., wage-price controls may be unlawful)
    

---

## **8. Negotiable Instruments & Commercial Paper**

- **Negotiable Instrument Requirements:**
    
    - Signed, unconditional, payable to order or bearer, fixed amount, on demand or at a definite time
        
- **Indorsement:** Signature of holder transferring rights
    
- **Transferee of Non-Negotiable Paper:** Gains rights subject to original contract, not holder-in-due-course protections
    

---

## **9. Consumer & Data Protection**

- **FTC Rules:** Protect consumers in credit transactions; lenders must provide disclosures
    
- **GDPR:** Individuals have the right to request deletion of personal data
    
- **Consumer Product Example:** Blender not working as advertised may trigger implied warranty claim

## **10. Criminal Procedure**

- **Arrest & Probable Cause:** Officer must have cause and follow lawful procedures
    
- **Field Sobriety Tests:** Officer may conduct if reasonable suspicion exists
    
- **Search Warrants:** Required for lawful searches of private areas, except open areas
    
- **Burden of Proof:**
    
    - Criminal: Beyond a reasonable doubt
        
    - Civil: Preponderance of evidence

## **11. Miscellaneous Topics**

- **Line-Item Veto:** Power to nullify specific provisions of legislation
    
- **International Law:** Torture is universally prohibited (post-WWII conventions)
    
- **Contracts Across Borders:** Parties can include choice-of-law clauses
    
- **Ethical Dilemmas in Business:** Balance between company interests, employee well-being, and societal impact

## **Week 8 – Business Entities & Corporate Governance**

### **1. Labor Law / National Labor Relations Act**

- **Scenario:** Bell Production union election
    
    - Facts: 12/15 employees signed union cards; employer gives benefits to the 3 non-union employees; only 3 voted for union.
        
    - **Rule:** It is **illegal for an employer to interfere with union organizing** by granting selective benefits to discourage union membership.
        
    - **Analysis:** Company likely **violated the NLRA** by incentivizing non-union employees to not join.
        

---

### **2. Corporate Structures**

- **S Corporations (S Corps):**
    
    - Created to encourage small-business entrepreneurship.
        
    - Pass-through taxation (profits/losses reported on shareholders’ personal returns).
        
    - Limited liability for shareholders.
        
- **LLCs (Limited Liability Companies):**
    
    - Flexible structure (cross between partnership and corporation).
        
    - Can have corporate or foreign investors.
        
    - Members can assign economic interests, but sometimes **cannot assign ownership without consent** (check operating agreement).
        
- **Professional Corporations (PCs):**
    
    - Typically used for licensed professionals (lawyers, doctors).
        
    - Liability limited to entity for most actions, but malpractice liability may attach to individual professional.
        
- **General Partnerships:**
    
    - Operate without filing paperwork; partners have **joint liability**.
        
    - Self-dealing by a partner (e.g., secretly bidding via LLC) can be actionable by other partners.
        
- **Corporate Governance:**
    
    - Director continuing after losing reelection = “**holdover director**.”
        
    - Shareholder approval required for: mergers, fundamental corporate changes.
        
    - Nonbinding shareholder vote on executive pay = “**say on pay**.”
        
- **Piercing the Corporate Veil:**
    
    - Can occur if a single member mismanages funds or ignores corporate formalities.
        
    - Creditor may sometimes recover directly from owner if veil pierced.

### **3. Fiduciary Duties**

- **Managers & Officers:**
    
    - Duty of care, loyalty, and good faith.
        
    - **Self-dealing:** Manager benefits personally at the expense of company → breach.
        

---

### **4. Business Formation Recommendations**

- **Startups seeking venture capital:** Incorporate as **C Corp** for ease of raising funds.
    
- **Small partnerships without LLC option:** Form as **general partnership or PC** depending on profession.
    
- **Sole proprietorship advantage:** Simplicity, direct control, pass-through taxation.

## **Week 9 – Securities, Bankruptcy, and Antitrust**

### **1. Securities Law**

- **Investing in registered vs. unregistered securities:**
    
    - Verify SEC registration; private investments may be risky.
        
- **Insider trading:**
    
    - Trading on material nonpublic information (e.g., overhearing a corporate acquisition) → illegal.
        
    - Even if info overheard publicly, liability may exist depending on relationship and knowledge.
        

### **2. Bankruptcy**

- **Chapters Overview:**
    
    - **Chapter 7:** Liquidation; debts discharged, assets sold.
        
    - **Chapter 11:** Reorganization; company proposes plan.
        
    - **Chapter 12:** Farmers keep farm while restructuring debt.
        
    - **Chapter 13:** Individual repayment plan; court approval required; plan must be feasible and in good faith.
        
    - **Subchapter 5:** Small business reorganization.
        
- **Non-dischargeable debts:**
    
    - Certain taxes, student loans, fraud-related debts, child support, some recent medical debts may be dischargeable depending on limits.
        
- **Bankruptcy Process:**
    
    - Trustee collects and distributes assets.
        
    - Filing involuntary petition requires agreement of creditors (number and type of creditors matter).
        

### **3. Antitrust Law**

- **Tying arrangements:** Forcing buyer to purchase unwanted goods along with desired ones → may violate antitrust law.
    
- **Standard Oil → Sherman Antitrust Act**: Restrains monopolistic practices.
    

---

## **Week 10 – Consumer Protection & Insurance**

### **1. Credit & Debt**

- **Credit Card Liability:**
    
    - Liability for unauthorized charges is limited once cardholder notifies issuer.
        
    - Timely reporting is key.
        
- **CARD Act:**
    
    - Protects consumers from unfair credit card practices.
        
    - Requires clear disclosures, limits interest rate increases, and protects minors.
        

### **2. Insurance**

- **Types:**
    
    - **Life insurance:** Term vs. whole life
        
    - **Annuities:** Owner receives payments over time
        
    - **Key person insurance:** Protects company from loss of valuable employee
        
    - **Liability insurance:** Protects against property damage or personal injury (e.g., rocketry incident)
        
    - **Auto insurance:** Collision, comprehensive (fire, theft, vandalism)
        
- **Insurance Covenants:**
    
    - Bad faith = refusal to accept reasonable settlement
        
    - Misrepresentation/fraud = coverage denied
        

### **3. FTC / Consumer Protection**

- **FTC Act §5:** Prohibits unfair or deceptive acts
    
- **False advertising:** Claiming medical benefits without randomized controlled trials violates law

## **Week 11 – Environmental Law & IP**

### **1. Environmental Regulation**

- **EPA Permits:** Required for discharges into navigable waters.
    
- **Clean Water Act:** Point source vs. nonpoint source pollution.
    
- **Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA):** Regulates hazardous waste; penalties for violations.
    
- **CERCLA / Superfund:** Liability for cleanup of contaminated sites.
    
- **Endangered Species Act:** Federal agencies must consult to avoid harming protected species.
    
- **Toxic Substances Control Act:** Regulates new and existing chemicals.
    
- **Environmental Impact Statements:** Required for major federal actions affecting environment.
    

### **2. IP & Patents**

- **Patents:**
    
    - Application process = **patent prosecution**
        
    - Patentable subject matter: new, useful, non-obvious inventions
        
    - Synthetic genes may be patentable if not naturally occurring
        

---

## **Week 12 – Bailments, Property, Landlord/Tenant, Trademarks, and Contracts**

### **1. Bailments**

- **Definition:** Delivery of personal property to another for a purpose under a contract.
    
- **Duty of care:** Varies by type of bailment:
    
    - Sole benefit of bailee → higher standard
        
    - Sole benefit of bailor → lower standard
        
    - Mutual benefit → ordinary care
        

### **2. Landlord/Tenant**

- **Landlord duties:** Deliver possession, maintain habitability
    
- **Tenant obligations:** Pay rent, not damage property
    
- **Periodic tenancy:** Lease automatically renews unless notice given
    

### **3. Gifts**

- Must intend to transfer ownership; delivery required
    
- Engagement ring: Conditional gift if tied to marriage
    

### **4. Trademarks**

- **Lanham Act:** Protects registered marks; prior user without registration may have limited rights
    
- Trademark infringement: Likelihood of confusion is key factor
    

### **5. Contracts & Warranties**

- **Implied warranty of habitability:** Landlord must ensure rental property is safe and livable
    
- **Product warranties:**
    
    - Express = explicit promise
        
    - Implied = fit for ordinary use
        
    - Misleading advertising → legal liability
        

### **6. Insurance & Risk**

- Bailment insurance: Protects bailee from losses
    
- Key person / business insurance protects company from financial losses due to loss of vital employee
