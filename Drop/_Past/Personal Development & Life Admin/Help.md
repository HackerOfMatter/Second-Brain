## 🧭 Step 1: Shift Your Mindset — From “Note-taking” to “Signal-hunting”

When you’re in class or reading, you’re not trying to capture _everything said_ — you’re trying to extract **signal**:

- Key concepts
    
- Relationships
    -
- Causes and effects
    
- Definitions that change how you think
    

### 🔹 Mini mental filter (use this while listening):

Ask yourself after every point:

> “Would I need to recall, explain, or apply this on an exam, project, or real-world example?”

If yes → it’s a **keeper**.  
If no → skip it or make a one-line reference in the margin (“see example in Ch. 4”).

## ✋ Step 2: Structure the Chaos — “3-Zone Notecard System”

Each card has _zones_ that tell you how to split ideas without wasting space.

|Zone|Purpose|Example (Accounting Lecture)|
|---|---|---|
|🧠 **Concept Zone** (top 1/3)|The _main idea_ or question this card answers|_“What is the matching principle?”_|
|📊 **Core Details Zone** (middle)|Facts, formula, or explanation|_Expenses are recognized when incurred, not when paid._|
|🔗 **Connections Zone** (bottom 1/3)|Related terms or examples|_Links to: Accrual Accounting, Adjusting Entries_|

➡️ **One card per concept**, not per fact.  
If you find you’re cramming too much: split it into sub-cards that answer _different questions_.  
If you find you’re writing single definitions only: combine a few _closely linked_ ones into one “cluster card.”

---

## 🧩 Step 3: Refine Efficiently — “Obsidian Funnel” Method

You don’t need to rewrite every card.  
Instead, think of Obsidian as your _refinery_ where rough ideas get organized into reusable gold.

### After class or weekly:

1. Lay out your cards.
    
2. Pick out the 20–30% that feel **foundational** or **confusing**.
    
3. Transfer those into Obsidian using this structure:
    
# {{Concept Title}}

**Core Idea:** 
- {{definition or summary from card}}

**Example/Application:** 
- {{real-world or class example}}

**Related:** [[Matching Principle]] → [[Adjusting Entries]]

**Questions to Review:** 
- When is revenue recognized vs. expenses?
`

💡 Tip: use `[[links]]` instead of trying to nest every note. Links build _connection thinking_ automatically.

You’re not retyping all notes — you’re **curating** what’s worth keeping long-term.

## 📚 Step 4: Organize Raw Data With Purpose — “Context Buckets”

If you get messy streams of info (dates, numbers, examples), route them into buckets:

- **Concept** — the “why” (goes on card and into Obsidian)
    
- **Example** — the “what” (may stay in raw notes or attach as subnote)
    
- **Procedure** — the “how” (belongs in Obsidian as a checklist/template)
    

Example:

> In lecture you hear: “We adjust Depreciation Expense at year-end to match cost with benefit.”  
> → Card: _Matching Principle_  
> → Example: stays as a note under “Depreciation Adjusting Entries” in Obsidian.

---

## 🧠 Step 5: Rehearse Through Retrieval — “Two-touch Review Cycle”

When you review:

1. **Touch 1 (Card):** Recall actively — quiz yourself from the question side.
    
2. **Touch 2 (Obsidian):** Read through linked notes once a week, connecting ideas or updating examples.
    

This way, cards strengthen _memory_, Obsidian strengthens _understanding_.

---

## 🩹 The Cure (Summarized)

|Problem|Cure|
|---|---|
|Too much info, don’t know what matters|Ask “Would I need to _use_ this?” — only keep applied concepts.|
|Cards get cluttered|Use the 3-zone layout and one concept per card.|
|Don’t want to waste paper|Combine _related micro-concepts_ into one cluster card.|
|Hard to transfer to Obsidian|Only refine top 20–30% of cards (core or confusing).|
|Notes messy|Route info into Concept / Example / Procedure buckets.|
