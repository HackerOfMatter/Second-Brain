## ⏳ Payback Period Method

### **Definition**

- Time required for project cash inflows to **recover the initial investment**.
    

### **Formula**

Payback Period=Years before recovery+Unrecovered cost at start of yearCash flow during year\text{Payback Period} = \text{Years before recovery} + \frac{\text{Unrecovered cost at start of year}}{\text{Cash flow during year}}Payback Period=Years before recovery+Cash flow during yearUnrecovered cost at start of year​

### **Advantages**

- Simple to calculate
    
- Emphasizes liquidity (useful for risky environments)
    

### **Disadvantages**

- Ignores time value of money
    
- Ignores cash flows after payback period
    
- Arbitrary acceptance standard (no link to shareholder value)
    

---

## ⚖️ Arbitrary Standard

- **Definition:** A decision rule (e.g., “accept projects that pay back in < 3 years”) without theoretical justification.
    
- **Problem:** Doesn’t ensure value creation; just a rough risk filter.

## 🔄 Alternative Approaches to Compare Projects

|Approach|Description|When to Use|
|---|---|---|
|**Discounting Approach**|Discount all cash flows (both inflows and outflows) to **time 0** and compare PVs.|When timing differences exist.|
|**Reinvestment Approach**|Compound all cash inflows to the **end of the project** at a reinvestment rate.|When assuming reinvestment at WACC or project return.|
|**Combination Approach**|Discount outflows to time 0 and compound inflows to the end of the project.|
