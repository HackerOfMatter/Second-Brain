## 🧭 Executive Summary
- **Core concept:** The Profitability Index (PI) measures the relative profitability of an investment by comparing the present value (PV) of future cash inflows to the initial investment.  
- **Why it matters:** It helps rank investment projects when capital is limited (capital rationing).  
- **Real-world relevance:** Commonly used by financial managers and analysts to compare competing projects with different costs.  
- **Likely use:** Decision-making tool in capital budgeting, especially under limited funds.

---

## 🧩 Key Concepts & Terms
| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Profitability Index (PI)** | Ratio of PV of future cash inflows to initial investment | PI = 1.25 → $1.25 return per $1 invested |
| **Present Value (PV)** | The current worth of future cash flows discounted at the required rate of return | PV = CF / (1 + r)^t |
| **Capital Rationing** | When a company has limited funds and must prioritize projects | Company only has $1M to allocate among proposals |
| **Mutually Exclusive Projects** | Choosing one project excludes others | Choosing Project A means rejecting Project B |

---

## 📊 Frameworks, Models, or Equations
| Model / Formula | Components | Interpretation | Example |
|------------------|-------------|----------------|----------|
| **PI = PV of Future Cash Flows / Initial Investment** | PV = Σ(CFt / (1 + r)^t) | > 1 = Accept, = 1 = Breakeven, < 1 = Reject | Project A: PV = 150,000; Cost = 100,000 → PI = 1.5 ✅ |

---

## 🪜 Step-by-Step Application
1. Estimate future cash inflows for the project.  
2. Choose an appropriate discount rate (cost of capital).  
3. Compute PV of future inflows.  
4. Divide PV by the initial investment to get PI.  
5. Compare to 1.0 threshold or rank projects by PI.

> 💡 *Interpretation:*  
> A PI greater than 1 means the project creates value; less than 1 means it destroys value.

---

## 🔍 Conceptual Deep Dive
- **Advantages:** Considers time value of money; useful for ranking projects; works well for capital rationing.  
- **Limitations:** Can conflict with NPV when projects are mutually exclusive; depends on accurate cash flow estimation.  
- **Related topics:** [[Net Present Value (NPV)]], [[Internal Rate of Return (IRR)]], [[Capital Rationing]]

---

## 📚 Real-World Example / Case Connection
- **Example:** A firm evaluating two software investments — one with high initial cost but long-term gains and one smaller short-term project. PI ranking helps prioritize under a $1M capital limit.
