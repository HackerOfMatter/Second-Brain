2.7 SUMMARY
It is important for financial planners to learn lessons from the psychological domain to help them foster deeper relationships with their clients to facilitate change toward financial well-being. This chapter introduced several psychological elements that can be integrated into a financial planner’s repertoire, but in the end what we hope this chapter accomplished was to pique interest in your clients’ internal worlds. We hope that you are now more curious about the origin story of your clients’ underlying attitudes, beliefs and cognitions. Potentially, you will be more likely to ask questions to help discover your clients’ money story so that you can formulate financial plans that are crafted to their individual needs. This is only the beginning, though, of the psychological elements of the client-planner relationship that are covered in later chapters.
